Priority fixes (WCAG 2.2 AA)

1. Images must have alternative text critical

33 elements on 1 page · WCAG 1.1.1 · rule image-alt

Who this affects: Blind users with screen readers hear nothing (or the file name) instead of what the image shows.

How to fix: Add an alt attribute describing the image, e.g. <img src="team.jpg" alt="Our four dentists in the clinic lobby">. Decorative images get alt="". Technical reference

2. Select element must have an accessible name critical

1 element on 1 page · WCAG 4.1.2 · rule select-name

Who this affects: Dropdowns with no label are unusable for screen reader users.

How to fix: Add a <label> connected to the <select>, or aria-label. Technical reference

https://www.w3.org/WAI/demos/bad/before/home.html

3. Links must have discernible text serious

7 elements on 1 page · WCAG 2.4.4, 4.1.2 · rule link-name

Who this affects: Screen reader users hear "link" with no idea where it goes. Very common on icon-only links (social icons, logos).

How to fix: Give the link visible text, or add aria-label="Visit our Instagram" to icon links. Technical reference

4. Elements must meet minimum color contrast ratio thresholds serious

2 elements on 1 page · WCAG 1.4.3 · rule color-contrast

Who this affects: People with low vision or colour blindness, and anyone reading on a phone in sunlight.

How to fix: Darken the text or lighten the background until the contrast ratio is at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text). Check the colours in your theme settings or CSS. Technical reference

5. All touch targets must be 24px large, or leave sufficient space serious

2 elements on 1 page · WCAG 2.5.8 · rule target-size

Who this affects: People with tremors or limited dexterity, and most phone users, mis-tap small controls.

How to fix: Make tap targets at least 24x24 CSS pixels, or space them out. Technical reference

Recommended improvements (best practice)

Not strict WCAG failures, but they make the site noticeably easier to use with assistive technology.

1. All page content should be contained by landmarks moderate

22 elements on 1 page · Best practice · rule region

Who this affects: Content outside landmarks is harder to find with a screen reader.

How to fix: Place content inside <header>, <nav>, <main> and <footer> elements. Technical reference

What this report does and does not cover

This report combines automated testing with the open-source axe-core engine (the same engine used by Google Lighthouse and many professional tools) and an automated keyboard walk-through. Automated testing finds a large share of common issues; Deque, the maker of axe-core, reported that it identified about 57% of issues by volume across 2,000+ audits. It cannot judge everything: whether alt text is accurate, whether captions are correct, whether content makes sense with a screen reader, and some keyboard and timing behaviour need a human tester.

This report is not a legal opinion or a certification of ADA or WCAG compliance, and fixing these items does not guarantee that a business will not receive a demand letter or lawsuit. If you have received a legal notice, speak with an attorney. We do not sell or recommend accessibility overlay widgets; fixing the underlying website is the reliable approach.
